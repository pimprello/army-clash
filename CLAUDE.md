# Notes for Claude

This repo auto-posts Army Clash Shorts to YouTube for Rellz (the battle is paced to end close to 60 s and the video ends 3 s after it, `rules.video`). `.github/workflows/post-army-clash.yml` renders with `render.js` + `engine.js` and uploads with `upload.js`. It posts 4 Shorts a day, at 10:00, 14:00, 18:00 and 22:00 London time (`rules.schedule`; Rellz, 2026-11-14: "make army clash upload 4 a day but make them hella good make it way better because its only four"): the workflow wakes every 15 minutes all day and `slot.js` lets the first run after each slot starts post (up to 110 minutes late, since GitHub's timer is often late). Because there are only four, every video auditions its battle first (`rules.quality.audition`: the same line-up fought headless with several sets of battle dice, scored for drama, and the best one is filmed) and is encoded at higher quality (`rules.quality.crf`/`preset`). A manual run (Run workflow) always posts. There is no self-queuing any more. If an upload fails, the reason is shown as a GitHub annotation (readable via the check-runs annotations API).

When Rellz asks to change future videos ("add a unit", "use these colours", "make X rarer"):
1. Prefer editing `rules.json`. Add a new rule there if the request is a dial he'll likely want to turn again, then document it in `RULES.md`.
2. For new behaviour, change `engine.js` (simulation and drawing) or `render.js` (rules, titles, tags). Keep old seeds deterministic when a new option is off.
3. Test before pushing: `npm ci`, then render one frame or a full video with `SEED=123 node render.js` (needs ffmpeg), and check balance across many seeds for gameplay changes.
4. Don't send Rellz preview videos (he asked not to; they take too long). Test with still frames instead.
5. Commit and push to `main`. The next run checks out `main`, so the change applies from the next video. Say plainly that already-posted videos don't change.
6. Then post a video with the update straight away: start the workflow by hand (Run workflow, or the REST dispatch API; a manual run always posts), check that it uploads, and give Rellz the link (Rellz, 2026-11-10: "whenever i make an update like this you have to upload a video"). This extra post is on top of the 4 scheduled ones; only for updates Rellz asked for, never for the daily autopilot.
7. For every idea Rellz asks for, add four more of your own in the same spirit (spells, characters, effects...), and improve the details around it with your own judgement (Rellz, 2026-11-10: "whenever i suggested something you have to always add your own so you make 4 more of your own ... improve every little detail").

Line-ups, formations and maps rotate deterministically from the number of rows in `uploads.md`. Every second video (`rules.creative.every`) is instead a creative match-up invented from the video's seed by `creative.js` and balanced on the spot with quick test battles (`sim-worker.js` runs them in parallel). With `rules.combat` on (intelligent combat: melee moves, fewer bullets, roster cards), every 1v1 video is also tuned on the spot (`creative.tune`), starting from the last tuned size of that line-up and mode in `videos.jsonl`; battle modes (`rules.modes`) and skins (`rules.skins`) roll their own dice in `render.js`. With `rules.tribes` on, each army is made of tribes (their own dice, `seed ^ 0x7121BE`) whose stories come from past videos (`chronicle.js` reads `videos.jsonl`); with `rules.tribes.evolve` their upgrades go to weapon tier 5 (tier 4 = an element, its own dice `seed ^ 0xE7017E`) and each shows on the troops and on a card. With `rules.roster` on, 14 weak troops are retired and 11 new ones (moves `ARMY_CAST4`, drawings `drawCast4`) plus an upgraded Gravity Tyrant and Gravity Monk are in. Every troop has one of 19 classes (`rules.classes`, table `CLASSES` in `engine.js`, written out in `CLASSES.md`: passives, counters, a badge on the roster cards); a new troop needs a class. Troops have personalities and swear vengeance (`rules.minds`, no dice: from the troop's number), and every troop has pathfinding and crowd avoidance (`rules.nav`: grid flow fields, engagement rings, avoidance, unsticking); none of this may use the sim's dice or the video clock (`this.t`) for anything that changes the battle: use `fightT`. With `rules.titans` on, every army has 3 or 4 titans and 1 or 2 giant characters (their own dice, `seed ^ 0x717A45`; new big creatures in `ARMY_CAST5`/`drawCast5` and `ARMY_SP3`/`drawCast6`) and a fallen titan's tribes can buy the graveyard spell to raise it; with `rules.affinity` on, troops of an element (fire, ice, storm, acid: `SH_EL`, or their tribe's at tier 4) are healed and strengthened by it instead of hurt (`affOf`, `affFeed`). In the finale the camera keeps the falling troop centred and may go past the arena's edge, where `draw()` mirrors the floor (`camOut`); particles are clipped to the battlefield. With `rules.spells` on, the merchant sells from a spell book (`BOOK`, `ARMY_SP3` in `engine.js`: heal, rage, skeleton graveyard, acid flask, giant growth, shield dome, tornado, earthquake; no dice); `rules.titans.giants` adds giant characters; `rules.expendable` gives small troops less health (and with `crowd`, small troops in a crowd are targeted first, crushed and cleaved: `crowdTick`, `smallOf`, `cleave`; no dice, battle clock only); `rules.ui.clean` (camera framing, shot streaks, no overlapping names, thinner smoke) and `rules.ui.remains` (bones, ash, x-rays) are looks only, and so is `rules.ui.humans` (person-shaped troops drawn as little people: `HUMAN` table and `drawHuman` in `engine.js`; a new person-shaped troop needs a line in `HUMAN`) and `rules.ui.blood` (`ARMY_BLOOD`, colours in `BLOOD`/`BLOOD_OF`: blood jets, ground splatter painted into the stains layer, pools, spattered killers, fewer gunfire sparks). Spell and event areas (dome bubble, target rings, cones, flashes) are drawn on the ground under the troops (`drawSp3Under`, `drawCast5Under`, `drawEventsUnder`); only rims, light and flying things go over them. With `rules.ui.shine` on, troops are drawn on a layer of their own (`shineBegin`/`shineEnd` in `engine.js`) that gets gloss, damage marks by element and a rim light cut from the layer, then goes onto the screen; hits, explosions and deaths add particles, rings and screen effects (`matHit`, `shineBoom`, `shineDeath`, `shinePost`): looks only, and the battle never reads `u.ms`. Videos are drawn natively at 4K (`rules.video.scale` 2: `render.js` sets `RVB.RS`, the canvas is 2160x3840 with a 2x transform, and every pixel-level layer in `engine.js` (the shine layers, stains, background, ground copies, glitch effects) is sized and offset by `api.RS`); layouts stay in 1080x1920 units, and `check-layout.js` and still tests run at 1x. `rules.video.smooth` draws turning and battle-clock animations in between ticks during slow motion (put back right after drawing), smooths the shake and eases hit-stops. Every video opens with a short title card in the thumbnail's layout (`rules.intro`, in `render.js`: everything after it is shifted by its length), and screen notices never stack on top of each other (`rules.ui.noStack`: `noteBegin`/`noteFit` in `engine.js`; the shop and upgrade cards take turns). UI never jumps, in both games (Rellz, 2026-11-15: "all ui should be smooth, it should never glitch or teleport ... for both games"): banners, cards, pills, tags and names glide or fade to a new place (`glide()`, `rosterK()` in `engine.js`, also in Tower Clash's copy). Every video gets a thumbnail (`rules.thumbnail`, `drawThumb` in `engine.js`, `out/thumb.jpg`); `upload.js` sets it right after the upload and again once YouTube has processed the video (one set while YouTube is still processing a Short can be lost); a refusal is only a warning. After changing any overlay (roster card, tribes panel, story, outro line), run `node check-layout.js`: it must pass. The layout is `rules.ui` (2 = the Shorts-safe layout: keep everything clear of YouTube's buttons and title; the layout check enforces it). World events (`rules.world`: glitch, flip, mirror, blackout, time stop, blood moon, supersize, rave) roll their own dice (`seed ^ 0x3E1D7A`) and must hit both sides alike. Battle events (airdrops, airstrikes, meteors) use their own dice (`setupEvents`), and the cinematic `Director` in `engine.js` only changes the camera and pace: the battle plays out the same with or without it (the simulation always moves in fixed 1/120 s ticks).

Which game an update is for (Rellz, 2026-11-15): an update Rellz asks for in the Army Clash chat is for Army Clash only; one asked for in the Tower Clash chat is for Tower Clash only; one asked for in the Clash Auto Pilot chat (the daily autopilot, renamed "Clash Auto Pilot") goes to both games. So from an Army Clash chat, change only `pimprello/army-clash`. Tower Clash's `music.js` and `engine.js` started as copies of this repo's (its `engine.js` is this one plus an `api._` export line); they are only brought back in step when an update is meant for both games. Every video has an original soundtrack (`rules.music`, `music.js`: written from the seed, follows the battle's cues from `tracker()`, ducks under the effects; the camera's beat punches and the seamless loop fade are in `render.js`).

A daily scheduled autopilot run follows `AUTOPILOT.md` (health check, learn from `stats/summary.md`, add one new thing, log it in `CHANGELOG.md`). Keep that file's guardrails in sync with Rellz's instructions, and log manual changes in `CHANGELOG.md` too.

Limits to remember: Rellz wants 4 uploads a day, each made as good as possible (never raise it without him asking); YouTube allows 100 API uploads a day per project, and the repo must stay active (each upload commits to `uploads.md`).
